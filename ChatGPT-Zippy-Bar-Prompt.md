# ChatGPT prompt: Zippy holding the gold bar (sprite sheet)

One sprite sheet of Zippy holding a gold bar in his own hands, with his fingers really wrapped round it. Today the game draws his fists on top of a separate bar, which can never look fully real. With this sheet, the bar is in the art itself. The bar's front is left blank, and the game prints the right number on it (3, 5, 7, 15…).

## How to use it

1. Open ChatGPT and start a new chat (image generation must be available).
2. Attach these two images from this folder:
   - `zippy swap.png`, so ChatGPT draws the same Zippy, hanging the same way;
   - `number bricks.png`, so the bar is exactly the game's gold bar.
3. Paste the prompt below and send it.
4. Download the image and save it in this folder as `zippy bar.png`, then tell Claude.

## The prompt (copy everything in the box)

```
Create ONE sprite sheet for a cartoon children's math game: Zippy the monkey holding a gold bar in his hands.

Zippy must be EXACTLY the same character as in the attached sprite sheet zippy swap.png: the same face, orange-brown fur, cream face, hands and tummy, big round ears, curly tail and proportions, with the same bold dark-brown outline and soft cel shading with glossy highlights. Do not redesign him.

The gold bar must be EXACTLY the attached gold bar (number bricks.png): the same shiny gold trapezoid ingot with its big sloped front face, light top face, darker right end and dark outline. It is BLANK: no number, no letters, no marks, in every frame.

The most important rule: the bar's big front face must be fully visible in every frame, facing the viewer, with NOTHING over its middle. A number will be printed there later. Zippy holds the bar only by its two short ends: his fingers wrap round the left and right ends of the bar, and his thumbs may rest on the top face near the ends. No fingers, thumbs, paws, fur or shadows cover the middle of the front face.

Canvas and layout:
- Wide landscape image, 1536 × 1024, with a fully transparent background. If transparency is not possible, use one flat, solid, pure magenta (#FF00FF) background with no gradient or texture.
- 8 frames in a 4 × 2 grid: 4 equal columns and 2 equal rows. One Zippy per frame, centred in his cell, with at least 40 px of empty space around him. Nothing touches or crosses into the next cell.
- In every frame Zippy hangs by his tail exactly like in zippy swap.png: his tail rises from his back and curls at the top centre of the cell, at the same height in every frame (do NOT draw the vine). His body hangs upright below it, head up.
- Zippy is the same size in all 8 frames, and the same size as in zippy swap.png: his head is the same size in every frame.
- The gold bar is the same size in every frame: a bit wider than his head. It never covers his face.
- He faces the viewer, turned slightly to the viewer's right.
- No text, numbers, labels, frame borders, grid lines, motion lines, sparkles, stars or shadows. No vine, no balance, no background. No other objects.

The 8 frames, left to right, top row first:
1. Catch: the bar has just landed in his hands at chest height; his fingers close round its two ends; surprised and happy.
2. Hold: he holds the bar in front of his tummy by its two ends, with a big happy smile.
3. Talk: the same as frame 2, but his mouth is open as if he is talking.
4. Think: he holds the bar in front of him and looks down at it, curious, one eyebrow raised.
5. Show: he lifts the bar up in front of his chest to show it off, proud. His face stays fully visible above it.
6. Reach: he leans forward and down with both arms stretched, holding the bar out below him by its two ends, ready to put it down.
7. Put down: the bar is low in front of him, still held by its ends, his fingers starting to let go, focused.
8. Give back: he holds the bar out to one side with both hands, as if handing it back, with a sheepish smile.

Before you finish, check: 8 separate frames in a 4 × 2 grid; the same Zippy at the same size in every frame; the tail curl at the same height in every frame; the bar looks exactly like number bricks.png, is the same size in every frame, and is blank; in every frame his hands hold the bar only by its two ends and the middle of its front face is completely clear; the background is transparent; there is no text and nothing else.
```

## If something goes wrong (follow-up messages for the same chat)

- **Fingers or thumbs cover the middle of the bar:**
  > In every frame, move his hands to the two short ends of the bar, so the whole middle of its front face is clear. Keep everything else the same.
- **There is a number or writing on the bar:**
  > Make the bar completely blank, exactly like number bricks.png, with no number or writing. Keep everything else the same.
- **The bar looks different from number bricks.png:**
  > Make the bar exactly like the attached number bricks.png: a shiny gold trapezoid ingot with a big sloped front face. Keep the poses.
- **The bar changes size between frames:**
  > Draw the bar at the same size in all 8 frames, a bit wider than his head. Keep the poses.
- **The bar covers his face:**
  > Keep his face fully visible in every frame; hold the bar lower. Keep everything else the same.
- **Zippy hangs upside down, or looks different from the attached sheet:**
  > Make him hang by his tail exactly like in zippy swap.png (tail curling at the top, body upright, head up), and look exactly like him. Keep the poses and the bar.
- **The background is not transparent:**
  > Make the background fully transparent, or one flat pure magenta (#FF00FF). Keep everything else the same.
- **Zippy changes size between frames, or his tail moves up and down:**
  > Draw Zippy at the same size in all 8 frames, with his tail curl at the same height in every frame. Keep the poses.

## What happens next

Claude cuts the sheet into 8 frames and lines them up on the tip of Zippy's tail, like his other sheets. Then Claude marks where the bar's front face is in each frame, so the game can print the bar's number there, at the right size. Zippy then uses these frames whenever he holds a bar:
- catching it from the child (1–2);
- talking and thinking while the child checks it (3–4);
- showing it off (5);
- swinging over to swap it for the crown (6–7);
- handing a wrong bar back (8).

The old fists-over-the-bar layer is then no longer needed for the bar.
